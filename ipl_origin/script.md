# Script: The IPL Could Have Started in 1995

Source: ElevenLabs voiceover (2025-07-02), 2:44 runtime

---

## HOOK (0:00–0:05)
If BCCI had listened to Lalit Modi, the IPL would have started in 1995 or 1996.

## SETUP: MODI'S FIRST IDEA (0:05–0:34)
Yes. Lalit Modi had studied in America and had seen the NBA there.
So he thought something similar could be done with cricket in India.

We're talking about the 1990s, so T20 didn't exist yet.
So Modi put a proposal in front of BCCI for a 50-over tournament.
He told BCCI it would be called **Indian Cricket League Limited**,
with city-based teams in which both Indian and overseas players would take part.

## REJECTION (0:34–0:60)
But BCCI didn't show much interest in Modi's plan.
The reason: the plan was going to cost a lot of money.
Plus, BCCI didn't like that the teams would be owned by a third party, a private organisation.

So Lalit Modi's proposal was rejected.

Worth noting: at this point Lalit Modi wasn't an influential figure in cricket.
He was just a businessman.

## TURN: GETTING INSIDE THE SYSTEM (1:00–1:27)
From here, Modi understood that if he wanted to change the system, he had to become part of it.
And he had some useful resources:
- a business background,
- political connections,
- and a growing network among cricket administrators.

So Modi started pushing in every direction.

Fast forward to 2007, and things had changed a lot.
Lalit Modi was now Vice-President of BCCI. He had a position of power.

## THE THREAT: ICL (1:27–1:46)
Also in 2007, Zee Media founder Subhash Chandra launched a private T20 league: the **ICL, Indian Cricket League**.
It was an independent league. It had nothing to do with BCCI.
So it challenged BCCI's monopoly on Indian cricket.

And of course, BCCI didn't like that, and they weren't going to sit idly by.

## BCCI STRIKES BACK (1:46–2:13)
BCCI acted immediately.
- First, anyone who played in the ICL would be **banned from Indian cricket for life**.
- Kapil Dev was involved with the ICL, so he was removed as chairman of the NCA, the National Cricket Academy.
- BCCI told every state association not to host an ICL match in its stadium.
- And most importantly, BCCI finally decided to start its own T20 league.

## PAYOFF: IPL IS BORN (2:13–2:31)
Lalit Modi already had a plan ready.
The first T20 World Cup began on **11 September 2007**.
Just two days later, BCCI announced a franchise-based T20 league:
the **Indian Premier League, the IPL**,
starting in April the following year.

## MYTH-BUST (2:31–2:42)
A lot of people believe the IPL started because India won the T20 World Cup.
It's not like that. The IPL was planned long before.
